WHAT CHANGED IN THIS ROUND (v3)

Eleven corrections were made to the family record based on the
Descendants of Joshiah C. W. JORDAN file (Family Tree Maker, 2019,
111 pages) and one Ancestry profile match found via public web search.

The biggest finding: Hazel Mae Hall was born Hazel Mae Smith in Carbon
Hill, Walker County, Alabama on May 6, 1908. She was adopted by William
Green Hall and Pleasant Allie Farr. Her marriage certificate in 1927
was still filed under Hazel Smith.

The Maggie Allen Jordan mystery is resolved: she was Margaret Emily
Kincaid, James Thomas Jordan's second wife, born May 17, 1866 in
Garland County, Arkansas, died April 14, 1962 in Minneapolis at age 96.

The Burton line through Barbra Barton goes back to Thomas Burton, born
about 1630 in Salisbury, Wiltshire, England, married Susannah Hatcher.
That makes them Wayne's seventh great-grandparents.

The documented Jordan line now goes back to Joshiah C. W. Jordan, born
about 1771. The Samuel Jordan 1620 Virginia connection is family
tradition, with about 150 years of generations still unaccounted for
between Samuel and Joshiah.

The full list of changes lives in Sheet 1 of the spreadsheet.

THE 23ANDME / ANCESTRY QUESTION

Wayne's dad asked: "How come we can't reach into all that AncestryDNA
and 23andMe data and fill the gaps?"

The honest answer is that those services sit behind individual logins.
No outside system, including the one that compiled this batch, can
reach into a private account and pull match lists, DNA segments, or
private trees. What can be done is what was done here: take what the
family has uploaded, photographed, exported, or remembered, and stitch
it together with public records (Find a Grave, census indexes that are
public, town histories, the Melting Pot Genealogical Society Hot
Springs archives) and with public Ancestry profiles surfaced by web
search.

The workflow that works for the next round: Wayne logs into Ancestry,
pulls a specific record (the 1910 Carbon Hill Smith census page would
be the single biggest unlock), exports it as a PDF or screenshot,
uploads it to a new conversation, and gets it transcribed and
integrated into the spreadsheet and the HTML.


PROJECT TONE

The site is intentionally written in an antiquarian heritage register,
not in a corporate or sales voice. Every story is told in the order it
happened. Every fact is sourced. Every gap is acknowledged. The motto
on the wax seal — Percussa resurgo — comes from Samuel Jordan's 1620
Virginia plantation and means "struck down, I rise again."

The Bible is the through-line. Eight generations have written in it.
Sylas Rayne Jordan will be the ninth.
