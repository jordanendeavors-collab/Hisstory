Jordan— his story —His Story

A family in one book, since 1858.

In the spring of 1620, a man named Samuel Jordan stepped onto the south bank of the James River in Virginia and patented a plantation he called Beggars Bush. The settlement came to be known as Jordan's Journey. Two years later, when the Powhatan rose against the English colonies on the morning of March 22nd, 1622, Jordan's Journey was one of only eight places permitted to remain occupied. Samuel died the following year. His widow, Cecily, kept the plantation alive.

The Jordan family motto — Percussa resurgo — has been carried down through four centuries since. Struck down, I rise again.

This page is the public face of a much larger family record. The story below belongs to the broader history of America. The records behind the seal belong to the family itself.

The Bible

In 1858, the American Bible Society of New York printed a New Testament. Around that same year — likely as a wedding gift, or as the first major purchase of a young household — a copy entered the home of Jonathan Worthy Jordan and his bride Margarett Caroline Roberts, married September 11, 1857.

That Bible is still in the family. It has been written in continuously for one hundred and sixty-eight years. Births, marriages, and deaths have been recorded in seven distinct hands across five generations. It survived Reconstruction, two world wars, the Dust Bowl, and the migration of the family from Arkansas to Los Angeles to the Sierra foothills of California. It was rebound, probably in the mid-twentieth century, in plain black leather with simple gold lettering. The original ornament is gone. What remains is the working book.

It now lives with the latest custodian, who is also the author of these words.

Why this exists

The genealogical work behind this site began as a private effort to read and verify what the Bible itself contains. It became something larger: a story about how a single book can hold a family together across centuries, across continents, across the gulf between one generation and the next.

The public side ends here. The full family record — the names, the dates, the open questions, and the Bible itself, photographed and transcribed — lives behind the seal below.

The Bible contains family entries written in at least seven distinct hands. The earliest is the patriarch Jonathan Worthy's, in faded brown iron-gall ink. The latest is the modern flyleaf where Clifton Wayne Jordan's name was inscribed in May 1978.

The Bible is dedicated by its present custodian to Sylas Rayne Jordan, the eighth generation. The current custodian and Sylas are the last two Jordans in this particular bloodline.

Open Questions

Gaps that remain in the record. To be resolved with the next photograph batch, or with Dad's memory.

  • The Old Testament title page — the most important missing photograph. Likely confirms the "1862" date that gives the project its name.
  • Frontispiece / dedication inscription — if it exists, the original 1857–58 patriarch's hand would appear here. Possibly: "To Jonathan W. Jordan, on the occasion of his marriage."
  • Margarett Caroline Roberts' death date — we have her birth (Feb 22, 1834) but no death record.
  • Fred Raymond Jordan's birth date — implied from his July 1971 marriage to Paula Hutchings, but not directly confirmed in the Bible.
  • The Bonnie Marie surname line — cut off in every photo. Could be Frank, Frank-Thomas, or another.
  • Marriage date for Johnie + Donnie Lena — smudged on the Marriages page. Side-lighting may bring out the year.
